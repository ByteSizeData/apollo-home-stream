Apollo on the PC with the games. A client on every screen you play on. Pair once. About five minutes.

1

On your gaming PC

The machine doing the work: it runs the game and sends the picture out.

🖥 Windows

Paste this into Terminal or PowerShell:

winget install ClassicOldSong.Apollo

Or use the installer. Afterwards Apollo's settings open at https://localhost:47990 — set a username and password there.

2

On every screen you play on

Pick the ones you own. Each gets its own resolution automatically.

i Artemis is Android-only — it's built from Moonlight's Android app. Everywhere else you use Moonlight, which works with Apollo just fine; you only lose a few of Artemis's extras.

📱 Android phone or tablet

Artemis — the client made for Apollo. Matches your phone's exact resolution and refresh rate.

Get Artemis ↗

📺 TV box

Android TV, Fire TV, Shield — Artemis, same download. Apple TV — Moonlight from the App Store. LG and Samsung TVs have community Moonlight builds, but an Android box is less hassle.

Moonlight — free, official, works. Or VoidLink — a paid App Store app with the Artemis touch and resolution changes carried over. Search the App Store by name.

💻 Mac, Windows or Linux laptop

Moonlight — desktop builds for all three. (Mac: there's also an unofficial moonlight-macos build that looks nicer.)

3

Pair each device once

Open the client, pick your PC, and it shows a code:

4172

Type it into Apollo's settings on the PC (https://localhost:47990). Done — that device is trusted from now on.

⚠ Only the first device you pair gets full permissions. Every later one can see your games but not start them — pressing play does nothing. Open that device in Apollo's settings and turn on Launch Apps, Mouse Input and Keyboard Input.
4

Reach it from anywhere

Only for playing away from home. Do this last — get one client working at home first, so if something breaks you know it's the VPN.

🔒 Tailscale on everything

Same account on the PC and every device you travel with. Nothing on your router changes, nothing is exposed.

winget install --id Tailscale.Tailscale
Windows ↗ Mac ↗ iPhone ↗ Android ↗

⚡ Two things that bite

Pair by the Tailscale name, not the home IP — then one entry works at home and away.

Allow UDP 41641 on the PC. Without it Tailscale falls back to a relay capped near 5–10 Mbps: it connects, it just looks terrible.

🔌 Keep the PC awake

A wake-up packet can't reach the PC from a hotel. Switch to Always awake before you travel.

A streaming PC is only useful if it's awake when you reach for it. Pick how you want yours to behave — you can set it up either way, and switch whenever.

🪟 Windows

    🍎 Mac

      ⚠ Waking it from the road doesn't work the way you'd hope. A wake-up packet is a local-network broadcast — it can't travel across the internet to your house. So if you want the PC reachable while you're away, either leave it on Always awake before you go, or keep something small at home that stays on and can send the packet for you (another always-on machine on your VPN, or a router that supports it). Everything else about streaming works from anywhere; only the waking is local.
      i Letting the monitor switch off is fine. It saves the panel and doesn't touch your stream — Apollo builds its own display for whichever device you're playing on. It's the machine going to sleep mid-session that drops you.
