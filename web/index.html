Apollo goes on the PC your games are installed on. A client goes on every screen you want to play on. Pair them once and you're done — about five minutes.

1

On your gaming PC

This is the machine doing the work — it launches the game, encodes the picture on your GPU, and sends it out.

🖥 Windows

Paste this into Terminal or PowerShell:

winget install ClassicOldSong.Apollo

No winget? Download the installer instead. When it finishes, Apollo opens its settings in your browser at https://localhost:47990 — set a username and password there.

2

On every screen you play on

Pick the ones you own. They all connect to the same PC, and each gets its own resolution automatically.

i Why isn't Artemis on everything? Artemis is a fork of Moonlight's Android app, so it only runs on Android — phones, tablets, Android TV, Fire TV, Shield. Desktop versions are being worked on and there's no iOS version or timeline. Everywhere else you use Moonlight, which connects to Apollo perfectly well — you just miss some of Artemis's extras, like its resolution and input handling.

📱 Android phone or tablet

Install Artemis — the client built to match Apollo. It asks your PC for your phone's exact screen resolution and refresh rate.

Get Artemis ↗

📺 TV box

Android TV, Fire TV, Shield — same Artemis app, installed from the same page, or sideload the APK if your TV has no store.

Apple TV — Moonlight has a tvOS app on the App Store instead. LG and Samsung smart TVs have community Moonlight builds, though a cheap Android box is usually less hassle.

Get Artemis ↗

📲 iPhone or iPad

No Artemis here — it's an Android app. Two options that both pair with Apollo normally:

Moonlight — free and official, works fine. VoidLink — a paid App Store app built from Moonlight with the Artemis changes carried over, so it handles touch controls and resolution more like Artemis does. Search the App Store by name.

Get Moonlight ↗

💻 Mac, Windows or Linux laptop

Moonlight — desktop builds for all three, and it pairs with Apollo the same way. On a Mac there's also an unofficial moonlight-macos community build that looks nicer, if you don't mind installing something unofficial.

Get Moonlight ↗

🎮 Steam Deck

Install Moonlight from the Discover store in Desktop Mode, then add it to your library so it launches from Game Mode.

3

Pair each device once

Open the client, pick your PC from the list, and it shows a four-digit code:

4172

Type that code into Apollo's settings page on the PC (https://localhost:47990). That device is trusted from then on.

⚠ Worth knowing before it confuses you: only the first device you pair gets full permissions. Every device after that can see your games but can't start one — it just does nothing when you press play. Open that device in Apollo's settings and turn on Launch Apps, Mouse Input and Keyboard Input.
4

Reach it from anywhere

Only needed if you want to play away from the house. At home you're already done.

i Do this last, not first. Get Apollo talking to one client on your home network before you add a VPN — otherwise, when something doesn't work, you can't tell whether it's Apollo, the client, pairing, or Tailscale. One new thing at a time.

But once it's installed, pair using the Tailscale name. Tailscale gives your PC a name that stays the same at home and away. If you add the host in your client by that name, the one entry works everywhere and you never pair a second time. Pair by home IP instead and you'll be adding the PC all over again from the road.

🖥 On the gaming PC

Windows — paste this, or grab the installer:

winget install --id Tailscale.Tailscale
Windows installer ↗

📲 On your phone and tablet

Install it from the store, sign in, and leave it on. Both apps are free.

iPhone & iPad ↗ Android ↗

💻 On your Mac or laptop

The Mac app is a normal download and sign-in.

Mac app ↗

On Linux or a Steam Deck in Desktop Mode, the official one-liner:

curl -fsSL https://tailscale.com/install.sh | sh

🔑 Then: one account, one setting

Sign every device into the same Tailscale account — that's what puts them on one private network. Nothing on your router changes and nothing is exposed to the internet.

Then allow UDP 41641 through the firewall on the gaming PC.

⚡ If it connects but looks terrible

That's the relay, not your PC and not your GPU. When Tailscale can't open a direct path it quietly falls back to a relay that caps around 5–10 Mbps — enough to connect, nowhere near enough to look good.

Opening UDP 41641 is the fix. Once it goes direct it will carry as much as your home upload allows.

🔌 Don't forget the PC itself

A sleeping PC can't be woken from a hotel — wake-up packets don't cross the internet. Set it to Always awake on the Sleep & wake page before you travel, or leave something at home that can wake it for you.

A streaming PC is only useful if it's awake when you reach for it. Pick how you want yours to behave — you can set it up either way, and switch whenever.
